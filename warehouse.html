AI Warehouse Optimization

A friend from business school makes seasonal toys for retailers like Walmart. They were forecasting demand by hand every month. I built a dashboard that does it for them using three AI tools, backtested against four years of their sales data.

The Problem

Toy manufacturing is deeply seasonal. Christmas, summer, back to school — the spikes come every year but the size of each one is hard to call. My friend was making production decisions based on last year's numbers and instinct, which worked until it did not. Too much inventory after a slow season meant cash tied up in a warehouse. Not enough before Christmas meant missed Walmart orders.

The business was profitable enough to survive this inefficiency but they knew they were leaving money on the table every cycle. What they needed was a forecast they could actually trust, something more than a spreadsheet with last year's numbers bumped up by 10%.

How I Built It

I used three tools, each playing a different role. Prophet and TimeGPT handle the actual forecasting. ChatGPT wrote most of the dashboard code. I spent more time on the data and the product decisions than on the engineering.

Prophet
Facebook's open source forecasting library. Purpose-built for business time series with strong seasonality. Feed it a date column and a value column, it handles the rest.
TimeGPT by Nixtla
A forecasting API. Send your historical data, get a forecast back. No model configuration, no training. The AI equivalent of "just works." Free at this scale.
ChatGPT
Wrote the Streamlit dashboard, the data cleaning scripts, and the chart code. I described what I wanted and iterated from there. Fully vibe coded, no apologies.

The dashboard shows both forecasts side by side with the actual historical data so my friend can see where the two models agree, and where they diverge. When both agree, that is a confident forecast. When they split, it flags a month worth paying attention to.

Hurdles

Nothing about this was as clean as it sounds in hindsight.

The data was a mess Four years of sales records across multiple spreadsheets, inconsistent date formats, some months missing entirely, product names spelled differently across files. I spent the first four days just cleaning data before a single model could run. This is always the unglamorous part nobody talks about.
TimeGPT did not like sparse data A few product lines had gaps of two or three months with zero sales. TimeGPT interpreted those as actual zeros rather than missing data, which threw off its forecasts badly on those SKUs. Had to impute the missing values before sending them through the API, which took some back and forth with the docs to figure out.
The vibe coded dashboard broke constantly ChatGPT would write working Streamlit code and then on the next prompt introduce a bug that broke something earlier. I learned to test every addition before asking for the next one. By the end I had a decent mental model of the codebase even though I did not write most of it.
Getting the forecast trusted was harder than building it My friend looked at the first version and said it felt like a black box. They wanted to understand why it was predicting what it was predicting, not just what the number was. I added a simple annotation layer showing which historical peaks were influencing each forecast, which solved it. The technical problem was easy. The trust problem took longer.

What I Took From This

Pick the right tool, not the impressive one Prophet and TimeGPT are not flashy. They are purpose-built for one thing and they do it well. Using a general LLM for numerical forecasting would have been the wrong call. Knowing the difference is what mattered here.
Vibe coding is a legitimate strategy I did not write the Streamlit dashboard from scratch and I do not pretend I did. I described what I wanted, iterated with ChatGPT, and ended up with something that works and that I can maintain. The skill was knowing what to build, not memorising Streamlit syntax.
The user problem is always harder than the technical one Getting the forecast to run was two days. Getting my friend to trust it took two weeks of iteration. Every PM project has this gap between "it works" and "people use it." I underestimated it here and will not next time.
